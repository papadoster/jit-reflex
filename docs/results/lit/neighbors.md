# Literature check: NEIGHBORS (latency, action chunking, corrections between calls)

Checked 2026-09-27. Every row below was verified on its arXiv abstract page (https://arxiv.org/abs/<id>) or a proceedings page, fetched in this session. "Venue" is what the primary page says. If the page names no venue, the cell says "arXiv preprint". The "what they do" text is summarized from the abstract. Details that come from the paper body (HTML) are marked [HTML].

Our method in one line: frozen flow-matching chunk policy π (H = 8, RTC Kinetix benchmark). A learned world model predicts ô_k. Between calls we execute π(ô_k)[k] + clip(J_k (o_k − ô_k)), where J_k = ∂π(ô_k)[k]/∂o comes from autodiff. The policy is not trained or fine-tuned, and there is no learned correction head.

## Closest competitors to our claim (ranked)

Our claim in short: a training-free (π frozen, no correction head) per-step correction between calls that uses π's own Jacobian at world-model-predicted observations, on the RTC Kinetix benchmark.

1. **A2C2 (Sendai et al., arXiv:2509.23224).** Same problem, same benchmark, same per-step slot between calls, and a reported +23 pp over RTC on Kinetix. The contrast is clean: A2C2 learns a residual from expert (PPO) actions, while our correction is π's own first-order response and needs no expert and no action labels. Two risks. First, the original head uses "features from the base policy", and our bt-kinetix variant (and Agouzoul's Kinetix variant) does not. Second, Agouzoul reports A2C2 above 90% on Kinetix even at d = 8, well above our ~76%. The paper must state which A2C2 we ran, and why the numbers differ (H, s, levels, seeds) or where they agree.
2. **Real-Time EXPO-FT (Dong, Hung, Sadigh, Finn, arXiv:2609.18207, 16 Sep 2026).** A lightweight policy makes per-step reactive edits to a slow VLA's proposals. It is best in 10/10 Kinetix environments and strong on real hardware. It needs online RL (reward plus robot or sim interaction), which our method does not. It appeared 11 days ago and is very likely to be raised by reviewers. We should state the difference in supervision and, if possible, discuss results on overlapping levels.
3. **VLA-Feedback (Ji et al., arXiv:2609.21022, CoRL 2026).** The idea closest to ours: reuse the policy's own computation (its final denoising step) to fold in the latest observation per action without a full call. The difference is exactly our selling point. They run a network step at every action. We run the network only at call time (J_k is computed once per chunk) and apply a linear map per step, which also makes our correction interpretable as a feedback gain. Whether their last step is retrained is not stated in the abstract.
4. **DREAM-Chunk (Chen et al., arXiv:2606.18589).** Shares a frozen chunk policy, a learned world model at test time, and Kinetix. It is a selection method (sample K chunks, pick the one whose predicted latent future matches observations), targets stochastic dynamics rather than delay, and costs K policy samples. Ours is a single-sample, gradient-based correction. Moderate overlap, and it should be cited in the world-model paragraph.
5. **VLASH (Tang et al., arXiv:2512.01031) and FutureRTC (Jiang et al., arXiv:2607.24008).** Both anticipate the execution-time state (VLASH: proprioception by rollout plus fine-tuning; FutureRTC: learned visual and state forecast) and condition the policy on it. This is the "pred" family we already include as a baseline. They improve what π is called on, and neither corrects actions between calls. VLASH fine-tunes the VLA (per [HTML]), so "VLASH-like" should mean "re-query at the predicted state", not "VLASH".
6. **Pace-and-Path Correction (Zhang et al., arXiv:2605.11459).** The only other verified *training-free, closed-form* correction wrapping chunked VLAs. The correction is hand-designed geometry (pace and path channels) and not derived from the policy. Evaluated on MoveBench, not Kinetix. It weakens any "first training-free correction" wording, but not "first policy-Jacobian correction".
7. **Spatial Attention (Park et al., arXiv:2607.04739) and LPN (Xie, Karol, Hodgins, arXiv:2602.18312).** The only verified works using ∂action/∂observation of a policy. Spatial Attention uses it to set execution horizons, and LPN penalizes it during RL training to get smooth time-varying linear policies. Neither uses the Jacobian as a feedback gain to correct a stale chunk. These are the most important citations for the novelty claim "use the frozen policy's own Jacobian as the between-call feedback gain". Within this search, no paper does exactly that.
