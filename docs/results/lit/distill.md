# Literature check: is "A2C2-distill" already known? (checked 2026-09-27)

Our method, for reference: a small MLP head with the A2C2 architecture (input: current observation o_t, the stale chunk's action at index k, time feature of k; output: additive correction). It is trained by regression onto a* = pi(o_t)[0], a fresh call of the SAME frozen flow policy at the current observation, with the chunk's flow noise shifted by k. The training data are 1M transitions from the frozen policy's own naive chunked rollouts, with no expert and no reward. At test time it corrects every step between calls.

All entries below were checked on the arXiv abstract page (and, where stated, the arXiv HTML full text) or the named primary page. Author lists are copied from those pages. "Venue" is only what the primary page states; anything else is marked.

## Is distill known?

**Verdict: PARTIALLY KNOWN. The core mechanism has a concurrent close precedent. Our exact configuration was not found.**

- **Closest work: ForceDelta-VLA (arXiv:2609.18242, 16 Sep 2026).** Its "delay correction" branch trains a lightweight student that is conditioned on the stale reference chunk from an earlier query and on cache age. The student regresses onto the difference between the frozen teacher re-queried at the current state (with the cached noise ε_k) and the stale reference action, and the correction is added at deployment. This is essentially "distil the re-query into a cheap corrector conditioned on the stale plan". It was published 11 days before this check. Differences that remain for us:
  - Our re-query is a **full fresh call on the current observation**. Theirs keeps the vision-language prefix E_k cached and refreshes only state and force.
  - We use the **frozen policy's own naive chunked rollouts, with no demonstrations and no reward**. They use teleop demonstration trajectories.
  - We shift the flow noise by k. They reuse ε_k.
  - Our corrector is a standalone, A2C2-architecture baseline with no force channel and no Γ(S_t,S_k) state-alignment term.
  - We evaluate it in a latency-fair frontier against A2C2, RTC and the exact reflex. They do not compare to A2C2.
- **Also uses "the same frozen policy called on the fresh observation" as the teacher, but trains something else:**
  - DEFLECT (2605.19294): preference fine-tuning of the whole action expert.
  - FutureRTC (2607.24008): a consistency loss that trains an observation/state predictor, and a full call is still needed.
- **The A2C2 line (2509.23224, 2605.08168)** trains on expert or ground-truth targets. Neither proposes training on the base policy's own fresh outputs.
- **Residual RL (RPL, Residual RL, ResiP, ResFiT, Policy Decorator, RT-EXPO-FT, ARLI)** trains the residual with reward, not with self-distillation.
- **Whole-policy distillation (Consistency Policy, OneDP) and streaming (SDP)** distils the whole policy for speed and does not condition a corrector on a stale chunk.
- **Dual-system models (HiRT, RoboDual, GR00T N1, FiS, Helix, Hi Robot, RDP)** train the fast module end-to-end or on demos. None of those checked trains the fast module to imitate the slow policy's own fresh outputs.
- **Conceptual ancestors:** Policy Distillation (student regresses on a frozen teacher) and DAgger (teacher labels on states the learner visits). Our data come from the base's naive chunked rollouts, not the corrected policy's, so ours is a single non-iterated round rather than DAgger.

**Suggested wording for the preprint.** Do not claim "we propose self-distillation of re-queries". Write that the delay-correction distillation of ForceDelta-VLA and the fresh-observation teacher of DEFLECT and FutureRTC are the closest works. Then state the difference: ours needs no demonstrations or reward, re-queries the full observation with shifted flow noise, and runs as a drop-in A2C2 head evaluated latency-fairly. Cite ForceDelta-VLA as concurrent.
