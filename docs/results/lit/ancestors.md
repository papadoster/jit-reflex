# Ancestors in control and trajectory optimization: verified literature check

Checked 2026-09-27. Scope: sensitivity-based NMPC, RTI and MLI, DDP/iLQR/TVLQR/LQR-trees, and any prior work that uses a
learned policy's own input Jacobian as a feedback gain.

## How sources were verified

Publisher pages mostly refused automated fetches: SIAM (epubs.siam.org), SAGE, ScienceDirect and Springer returned 403 or
login redirects, IEEE Xplore rendered empty, and dblp returned 403/429 (Anubis bot wall). So each DOI was checked against
its registry record and an abstract source:

- **CR** = Crossref bibliographic query `https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=<title words + authors>` (this
  is the DOI registration metadata: title, authors, container, volume, pages, year, DOI).
- **OA** = OpenAlex record `https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:<DOI>` (used for the abstract where the publisher deposited one).
- **arXiv** = `https://arxiv.org/abs/<id>` (abstract page); **arXiv-html** = `https://arxiv.org/html/<id>` (used only to check a key method detail).
- Other landing pages (Optimization Online, SciTePress, PMLR, EPub Bayreuth, ar5iv) are named explicitly.

Issue numbers are given only where a fetched page showed them. "Abstract not retrieved" means that only the metadata was
verified, and the "what they do" text relies on the title plus a secondary snippet (marked).

Notation for our method (from the brief): at call time predict ô_k with a world model, J_k = ∂π(ô_k)[k]/∂o by reverse-mode
autodiff through the flow sampler, and between calls a_k = π(ô_k)[k] + clip(J_k (o_k − ô_k)).

## Closest prior art to "policy's own Jacobian as feedback between calls"

Ranked by how much each weakens the novelty claim.

1. **Merel et al. 2019, NPMP / linear feedback policy cloning (arXiv:1811.11711).** This is the most direct precedent for
   using a learned neural policy's own observation-Jacobian, obtained by autodiff, as a linear feedback gain around a
   nominal trajectory: μ_FB(s*+δs) = μ_E(s*) + J* δs. Section 3.1 also rolls this linear-feedback policy out under action
   noise next to the full expert and open-loop replay of the nominal actions. That is essentially our "stale replay vs
   Jacobian reflex vs full policy" comparison, seven years earlier, for single-step MLP experts. It does *not* address
   inference latency, action chunks, re-planning at each call, or world-model-predicted nominals, and its purpose is cheap
   distillation. **Consequence:** we cannot claim to be first to use a policy's Jacobian as a feedback gain. The claim
   has to be narrowed to a training-free Jacobian reflex for a *frozen action-chunking flow policy*, around *world-model
   predictions re-made at each call*, closing the loop between slow calls, with backprop through the flow sampler
   (including truncated-sampler variants) and a pre-registered evaluation. Cite LFPC prominently.

2. **Sensitivity-based multistep NMPC: Palma & Grüne 2012; Grüne & Palma 2015; Palma, Suardi & Kerrigan 2015;
   amsNMPC Yang & Biegler 2013; asNMPC Zavala & Biegler 2009.** Structurally this is our algorithm with an optimizer in
   place of π. One expensive solve produces a multi-step plan plus predicted states. Between solves, each planned input is
   corrected by (first-order sensitivity) × (measured − predicted state), and there are stability and robustness results
   as a function of how many steps run between re-optimizations. The whole "feedback from sensitivities between slow
   solves, around predicted states" idea is 2009–2015 NMPC. Our contribution relative to this line: the "solver" is a
   black-box learned generative policy with no KKT system and no internal model, so (a) the sensitivity must come from
   autodiff through the sampler, and (b) the predicted observations must come from a separately learned world model.
   These papers should be framed as *the* ancestors ("sensitivity-based multistep MPC, transplanted to a learned policy").

3. **DDP/iLQR feedback gains run between slow MPC updates, especially Grandia et al. 2019, "Feedback MPC"
   (arXiv:1905.06144),** and TVLQR/LQR-trees (Tedrake et al. 2010), with DDP (Mayne 1966; Jacobson & Mayne 1970) and
   iLQR (Li & Todorov 2004) as the source of u = ū + K(x − x̄). Grandia et al. is the robotics precedent for exactly
   "slow planner, fast time-varying linear feedback in between". Our difference: K is the policy's own Jacobian, not a
   Riccati gain, so there is no optimality or stabilization guarantee (see item 5).

4. **RTI / MLI / AS-RTI (Diehl, Bock, Schlöder 2005; Diehl et al. 2002; Bock et al. 2007; Frasch et al. 2012;
   Frey, Nurkanović & Diehl 2024).** Preparation-phase/feedback-phase splitting, a tangential predictor via
   initial-value embedding, reuse of stale linearizations at lower MLI levels, and (AS-RTI) linearization at a
   *predicted* state. Our "gradient through the last 3 of 5 flow steps" variants map naturally onto inexact-derivative
   MLI levels. Conceptual ancestors only; none involves a learned policy.

5. **Learned-policy sensitivity work that is not run-time feedback between calls:** Hose et al. 2024 (NN output +
   learned sensitivity × parameter deviation at run time, the closest run-time algebra but for parameters, not
   observations); Krishnamoorthy 2021 and Lüken et al. 2023 (sensitivities used for training data or Sobolev loss);
   TaSIL 2022 (Jacobian matching in training); Nakamura-Zimmerer et al. 2022 (accurate NN controllers can have
   non-stabilizing local linearizations, a caution directly relevant to trusting J_k).

6. **Recent chunking-policy feedback (2025–2026), outside the ancestor cluster but checked:** Tube Diffusion Policy
   (arXiv:2604.23609) uses the same tube/linearization framing but *learns* the feedback flow. FBFM (arXiv:2607.29235)
   is training-free and uses VJPs through the flow, but re-runs the generator with fresh observations inside the chunk
   instead of precomputing an observation gain. DCDP (arXiv:2603.01953) uses a learned correction module. RWM
   (arXiv:2505.15589) uses world-model predictions as references with an online-trained reflex. In the abstracts and
   HTML checked, none precomputes the frozen policy's observation-Jacobian at call time and applies it as a linear
   correction between calls. TDP and FBFM are the two a reviewer is most likely to raise and should be cited and
   contrasted.

**Overall assessment.** The mechanism (nominal + sensitivity × deviation from a predicted trajectory between expensive
solves) is classical: asNMPC/amsNMPC, sensitivity-based multistep MPC, RTI, and DDP/TVLQR gains. Its application to a
learned neural policy's own Jacobian already exists in LFPC (Merel et al. 2019), including closed-loop rollouts against
open-loop replay. What we found no precedent for is the specific combination: a frozen action-chunking flow/VLA-style
policy; the gain obtained by reverse-mode differentiation through the flow sampler (and truncated variants); nominal
observations from a learned world model, re-predicted at each call; applied between slow calls to fight inference
latency; training-free. The paper should present itself as "sensitivity-based multistep MPC / LFPC-style linear feedback
applied to frozen chunking generative policies", not as a new kind of controller.
